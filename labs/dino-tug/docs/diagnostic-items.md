# 사전·사후 진단 문항 / Pre/post diagnostic items

사전·사후에 같은 문항을 사용하되 문항 순서와 보기 순서는 각각 섞습니다. 섞은 뒤에도 아래 보기 키와 정답 대응을 유지합니다. 학생용 배부본에는 정답·코드·채점 설명을 숨깁니다. 이 문항은 앱 안에는 들어 있지 않습니다. 두 단계를 모두 선택하게 하고 사전 검사 직후 정답을 알려 주지 않습니다. 수치는 문항용 가상 조건이며 실제 사람·동물의 측정값이 아닙니다.

공통 채점: 1단·2단 모두 정답 = **과학적 개념**; 1단만 정답 = **이유 불명확**; 1단 오답 + 2단이 오개념 보기 = **해당 오개념 코드**; 그 밖(무응답 포함) = **기타**. 코드는 이 초안 안에서만 쓰는 분류 기호이며 검증된 척도가 아닙니다. 각 문항의 오개념 보기 옆에 코드를 적었습니다.

장력 문항은 질량을 무시하는 팽팽한 줄, 마찰 문항은 수평 바닥을 가정합니다. 힘은 지정한 한 물체에 작용하는 힘끼리 비교합니다. 정지마찰의 조절과 한계, 동물의 당김 한계는 앱의 단순화된 모형 범위에서 다룹니다.

## English

**Draft — review by a classroom teacher or researcher is required. These are not validated items.**

Use the same items before and after instruction. Shuffle item order and option order independently, keeping the keys and answers matched. Hide answers, codes, and scoring notes on student copies. These items are not part of the app. Require both tiers; do not reveal answers immediately after the pretest. Numbers are hypothetical conditions, not measurements of people or animals.

Common scoring: both tiers correct = **scientific concept**; only Tier 1 correct = **reason unclear**; Tier 1 incorrect with a misconception option in Tier 2 = **that misconception code**; all other responses, including omissions = **other**. Codes are local draft categories, not a validated scale. Assume a taut rope with negligible mass for tension, and a horizontal surface for friction. Compare forces on the specified single object. Static friction and animal pulling limits follow the app's simplified model.

### Item 1 — Concept 1
Target Concept 1: **Force has size and direction.**

- Target misconceptions: force is only present or absent (M1).
- Scene: A small ring on a rope is pulled left with 24 N and right with 36 N. It starts at rest. There are no other horizontal forces.
- Tier 1 — Just after release, how does the ring's motion change? A. Toward the left. B. Toward the right. C. It stays at rest.
- Tier 2 — Why? A. Both sides have force, so their effects are equal (M1). B. The difference between the opposite forces leaves a net force to the right. C. Since the ring was at rest, there were no pulling forces (M2).
- **Tier 1 answer: B / Tier 2 answer: B**
- Scoring: both correct=scientific concept; Tier 1 only=reason unclear; Tier 1 incorrect and Tier 2 A=M1, C=M2; otherwise=other.

### Item 2 — Concept 2
Target Concept 2: **Equal forces in opposite directions cancel out (balanced forces).**

- Target misconceptions: no motion means no force (M2); the rope is too taut to move (M2T, design §5 N1).
- Scene: Two teams of four hold opposite ends of a rope. The only horizontal forces on its center ring are 160 N left and 160 N right. The ring is at rest.
- Tier 1 — If these pulls continue, what happens to the ring? A. It stays at rest. B. It starts moving left. C. It starts moving right.
- Tier 2 — Why? A. Being at rest makes both pulls disappear (M2). B. The rope's tautness itself prevents motion (M2T). C. The opposite forces produce zero net force.
- **Tier 1 answer: A / Tier 2 answer: C**
- Scoring: both correct=scientific concept; Tier 1 only=reason unclear; Tier 1 incorrect and Tier 2 A=M2, B=M2T; otherwise=other.

### Item 3 — Concept 3
Target Concept 3: **Unequal forces change motion toward the larger force.**

- Target misconceptions: one side pulling hard guarantees victory (M3S); a small increase changes nothing (M3L).
- Scene: A rope marker starts at rest. The red team exerts 210 N left and the blue team 210 N right on it. Only red increases its pull slightly, to 225 N. Ignore other horizontal forces on the marker.
- Tier 1 — How does its motion change immediately? A. Toward the right. B. It stays at rest. C. Toward the left.
- Tier 2 — Why? A. The difference produces a net force of 15 N left; the forces are unbalanced. B. Red pulling hard guarantees a win regardless of blue's force (M3S). C. Adding just 15 N cannot change the balance (M3L).
- **Tier 1 answer: C / Tier 2 answer: A**
- Scoring: both correct=scientific concept; Tier 1 only=reason unclear; Tier 1 incorrect and Tier 2 B=M3S, C=M3L; otherwise=other.

### Item 4 — Concept 4
Target Concept 4: **Tension is the same all along a single rope.**

- Target misconceptions: tension is the sum of both pulls (M4S); tension is greater near the winning team (M4W).
- Scene: Tension gauges are near both ends of one rope with negligible mass. The rope is taut as the left team drags the right team. The left gauge reads 180 N. Gauge mass is also negligible.
- Tier 1 — What does the right gauge read at that instant? A. 360 N. B. 180 N. C. Less than 180 N.
- Tier 2 — Why? A. A gauge adds the pulls at both ends (M4S). B. Tension is higher near the winning team (M4W). C. Tension does not vary with position along this light rope.
- **Tier 1 answer: B / Tier 2 answer: C**
- Scoring: both correct=scientific concept; Tier 1 only=reason unclear; Tier 1 incorrect and Tier 2 A=M4S, B=M4W; otherwise=other.

### Item 5 — Concept 5
Target Concept 5: **Friction acts only as much as needed, up to a limit.**

- Target misconceptions: friction is always at its maximum (M5M); friction exists only during motion (M5O).
- Scene: A team holds a rope on a horizontal floor. Its maximum static friction is 240 N. The rope pulls the team right with 90 N, but the team stays at rest. Floor friction is the only other horizontal force.
- Tier 1 — What is the friction on the team now? A. 90 N left. B. 240 N left. C. 0 N.
- Tier 2 — Why? A. The floor provides no friction while the team is still (M5O). B. Before slipping, friction adjusts to balance this pull. C. Contact with the floor always produces the limiting friction (M5M).
- **Tier 1 answer: A / Tier 2 answer: B**
- Scoring: both correct=scientific concept; Tier 1 only=reason unclear; Tier 1 incorrect and Tier 2 A=M5O, C=M5M; otherwise=other.

### Item 6 — Concept 6
Target Concept 6: **Pulling capacity is the smaller of muscle and friction limits.**

- Target misconceptions: a larger animal is always stronger (M6B); species name determines victory (M6N, design §5 Q7).
- Scene: Large model animal K and small model animal L play tug-of-war. K has a muscle limit of 900 N and a friction limit of 120 N. L's limits are 300 N and 260 N. Each pulls at its capacity. As in the app, capacity is the smaller of the two limits.
- Tier 1 — Which has greater pulling capacity? A. Large K. B. They are equal. C. Small L.
- Tier 2 — Why? A. A larger body pulls better regardless of the surface (M6B). B. K can transmit only 120 N, while L can transmit 260 N. C. Even with these numbers, we need the species names to decide (M6N).
- **Tier 1 answer: C / Tier 2 answer: B**
- Scoring: both correct=scientific concept; Tier 1 only=reason unclear; Tier 1 incorrect and Tier 2 A=M6B, C=M6N; otherwise=other.

## 기록 내보내기와 문항 결과를 합치는 방법 / Combining exported logs and item results

Press the teacher-mode box/down-arrow button (Export log) to download `dino-tug-log.json` before refresh or reset. The envelope contains `app`, `version`, `exportedAt` (export time, not response time), and `entries`. No names or student IDs are recorded; there is no network transmission or storage persistence. Records stay in device memory until the button is pressed.

Entries include only quests with completed observations, even if explanations are unfinished. Repeating a quest replaces its result with the latest completed attempt. There are no totals or performance-order fields. Score the separately administered pre/post responses by tier and category, then compare related quests by concept using `CONCEPTS_OF` in `src/62-curriculum.js`. Classify `pick:null` separately rather than as a correct prediction. Do not add identifiers to the app log.

This is **one device's record with an unknown number of students, not evidence of a learning effect**. Shared-device responses may be mixed and only the latest performance remains. A correct explanation after feedback is not an independent diagnostic answer. Pre/post changes alone do not establish a causal learning effect.
