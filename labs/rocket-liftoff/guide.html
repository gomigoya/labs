교사 모드에서 Space는 재생, 마침표는 한 프레임, Esc는 처음 상태입니다. 모든 모드에서 /는 UI를 숨깁니다. 예상 후보 선택은 학급 토론용이며 정답을 골랐다는 사실이 퀘스트 완료를 대신하지 않습니다. A/B는 화면 사진이 아니라 세계 좌표에 남는 점선 기록입니다. 같은 시점으로 두 조건을 비교하십시오.

과장 스케일과 근사·한계

아래 내용은 이론 카드 o절의 표현·결정 기록을 옮긴 것입니다. 화면의 벌어지는 대각선 도형(⤢)은 모형 비율임을 알립니다.

고도 압축 ⤢ — heightVis(h)=ln(1+max(0,h)/1000)/ln(2); 0 m→0, 1000 m→1 모형 단위 — 추정: 압축 기준 1000 m는 전시 설계값. 실제 고도·속도는 압축하지 않은 배열로 기록한다. 압축 좌표의 점 간격은 속력을 직접 비교하기 어려우므로 운동 점은 고정 선형 배율의 별도 시간 점 띠에도 둔다.

불꽃 길이 ⤢ — flameVis(T)=max(0,T)/6900000 — 추력의 선형 도해이고 실제 배기 불꽃 길이 예측이 아니다.

로켓·발사대·노즐·천체 구 ⤢ — 모형 형상 — 실제 크기 비율·지구와 달 사이 거리를 나타내지 않는다. 힘 화살표는 서로 같은 눈금을 유지한다.

수직 단일 단계, 일정 추력·배기 속도·중력, 공기저항 없음. 고도에 따른 중력 감소, 진공 추력 변화, 단 분리, 자세·궤도는 계산하지 않는다. 지구와 달 선택은 중력만 바꾼다.

질량·추력·배기 속도는 새턴 V 첫 단을 연상시키는 교육용 단순화이며 실제 기체 재현이 아니다.

추정: 착지는 반발 없이 멈춘다. 실제 착륙 장치·충격·파손은 다루지 않는다. 이 가정은 코드와 문서에 함께 표시한다.

짐 이름의 모순: 명세의 열거대로 빈 홈=추가 0, 중간 블록=추가 200000, 큰 블록=추가 500000 kg로 확정한다. 기본 실행은 수치 요구사항대로 추가 0이다. Q4의 ‘보통→무거움’은 중간 블록→큰 블록으로 판정한다. 화면에는 이 이름을 적지 않는다.

명세의 ‘연료가 더 많았다면 엔진 하나도 결국 뜰 수 있다’는 오류다. 엔진 하나 추력 6900000 N < 건조 무게 9515700 N이므로, 연료량만 늘려도 어느 순간에도 무게를 이길 수 없다.

simulate는 고정 격자에서 해석적 정확 적분을 사용한다. 근사 수치 적분 대신 이 방식을 택해 지연 이륙·소진 사이에 생기는 스텝 의존 오차를 제거했다. 별도 사건 배열이 경계의 시각을 보존한다.

구현의 추가 압축: 로켓 위치는 heightVis 값을 다시 0.50·heightVis/(1+heightVis) 모형 단위로 제한합니다. 선형 운동 점은 0.00012 모형 단위/m, 창 범위 ±0.76이며 넘어가면 끊김 도형을 씁니다. 위치 점은 5 s 간격, 최대 9개입니다. 이 값들은 물리 상수가 아닌 전시용 추정입니다. 힘 화살표는 한 마디 6900000 N, 최대 6마디 이후 끊김을 표시합니다. 로켓 형상 배율 1.45 역시 실제 크기가 아닙니다.

콘솔의 점선 전체 곡선은 현재 입력에서 PHYS가 계산한 예상 궤적이며, 실선과 밝은 커서는 관찰한 부분입니다. 아직 점화하지 않은 장면의 점선은 측정 기록이 아닙니다. 소진 순간의 추력 불연속은 직선으로 잇지 않습니다.

When Does a Rocket Lift Off? — Teacher Guide

A wordless laboratory for comparing thrust, weight and pad support on one force scale.

For elementary grades 3–6. Use prediction, manipulation and observable evidence; equations are for teacher review.

Two 40-minute lesson plans

Demonstration: introduction/prediction 5 min → Q1–Q2 10 min → Q3 10 min → Q4–Q5 10 min → Q6/reflection 5 min.

Student exploration: introduction 5 min → paired Q1–Q2 10 min → Q3–Q4 10 min → Q5–Q6 10 min → compare predictions and results 5 min. Exchange operator and recorder roles. These timings are teaching suggestions.

Use student mode for the sequence, free mode for comparison, and teacher mode for projection. Teacher tools provide pause, one frame, quarter speed, layers, pin, spotlight, laser, class predictions and reset. Space toggles play, period advances a frame, Esc resets, and / hides UI. Only the optional teacher numeric layer displays numbers and units. Choosing an answer does not complete a quest. A/B pins world geometry rather than a camera image.

Six quests

1. Can one engine lift the rocket?

Prediction pictures and answer
High lift / slow lift / an empty tank still on the pad. The third picture is correct.
Teacher prompt
Does a flame alone prove that the rocket will lift?
Misconception and counterexample
A visible flame is not sufficient. Watch the tank empty while the rocket remains supported.
Demonstration and layer order
Earth, no extra cargo, one engine → prediction → force cells → cancellation/support → tank section → ignite → skip to burnout.
A/B pin suggestion
A ignition with full fuel; B burnout. Keep engine count, cargo, gravity and camera fixed.
Observable evidence
Position dots remain on the pad. Even dry weight exceeds one engine’s thrust. Completion requires observing burnout.

2. How many engines lift immediately?

Prediction pictures and answer
Three / four / five nozzles with an immediate pad gap. Five is correct.
Teacher prompt
How is being airborne now different from lifting immediately?
Misconception and counterexample
Four engines do not lift at ignition. Fuel loss later permits delayed liftoff.
Demonstration and layer order
Earth, no extra cargo → prediction → engine steps → threshold → force cells → ignite four, pause and pin → ignite five. The event shot shows nozzles and flames.
A/B pin suggestion
A four engines at ignition; B five at ignition. Then observe the delayed four-engine liftoff.
Observable evidence
Four lift at 14.633493 s; five at 0 s. Immediate lift requires initial thrust/weight strictly greater than 1.

3. Does constant thrust mean constant acceleration?

Prediction pictures and answer
Equal increases in dot spacing / growing increases / shrinking spacing. The second is correct.
Teacher prompt
Why do the gaps between equal-time dots grow faster?
Misconception and counterexample
The purple thrust stays constant while orange weight decreases and the velocity tangent steepens.
Demonstration and layer order
Earth, no cargo, five engines → prediction → tank section → force cells → equal-time motion → graph → ignite → inspect.
A/B pin suggestion
A ignition; B during the same burn. Change time only.
Observable evidence
During burning, a(t) exceeds a(0). Compressed altitude spacing is not a speed scale; use the linear motion strip and velocity tangent. Explicit inspection is required.

4. Does more cargo always require more engines?

Prediction pictures and answer
Add a nozzle / keep five with a smaller margin / stay on the pad. The second is correct.
Teacher prompt
Which cells change while thrust stays fixed?
Misconception and counterexample
The larger cargo still leaves a positive thrust margin with five engines.
Demonstration and layer order
Earth, five engines, medium cargo → prediction → record → pin → large cargo → cells → cancellation/support → inspect comparison.
A/B pin suggestion
A 200000 kg extra; B 500000 kg extra. Keep five engines and Earth gravity.
Observable evidence
Initial thrust/weight decreases from 1.109407 to 1.013493; both exceed 1. Two distinct runs and explicit comparison are required.

5. Does the Moon need the same engines?

Prediction pictures and answer
Five required / one sufficient / impossible. The second is correct.
Teacher prompt
Why does weight change while mass stays the same?
Misconception and counterexample
Compare Earth and Moon weight cells on a common force scale.
Demonstration and layer order
No cargo, one engine → prediction → section → cells → cancellation/support → threshold → ignite on the Moon.
A/B pin suggestion
A Earth; B Moon. Change gravity only.
Observable evidence
Initial lunar thrust/weight is 1.434094, permitting immediate lift. The selector changes gravity only.

6. What is the smallest immediate-lift bundle for large cargo on Earth?

Prediction pictures and answer
Large cargo with three / four / five nozzles lifting immediately. Five is correct.
Teacher prompt
How can you establish that a successful bundle is the smallest?
Misconception and counterexample
Compare conditions at ignition; delayed liftoff does not count as immediate lift.
Demonstration and layer order
Earth, large cargo → prediction → cargo section → engine steps → threshold → cells → cancellation/support → ignite five.
A/B pin suggestion
A four engines; B five, with identical cargo, gravity and ignition time.
Observable evidence
The selected count must equal enginesNeeded and initial thrust/weight must exceed 1. The minimum remains five.

Display exaggeration, approximations and limits

Height compression: heightVis(h)=ln(1+max(0,h)/1000)/ln(2); 0 m maps to 0 and 1000 m to 1 model unit before a further bounded mapping 0.50·heightVis/(1+heightVis). The 1000 m reference is an exhibition estimate. Physical arrays retain actual altitude and velocity.

Flame length: flameVis(T)=max(0,T)/6900000; it is a linear thrust diagram, not an exhaust-length prediction. Rocket, launchpad, nozzle and globe sizes are models, not true scale. The expansion glyph marks this distinction. Force arrows retain a common scale: one cell is 6900000 N; beyond six cells a break symbol appears. Rocket geometry scale is 1.45. Equal-time dots use 5 s steps, at most 9 dots, and a separate linear strip scaled by 0.00012 model unit/m with a ±0.76 display window and an overflow break. All are display estimates.

One vertical stage, constant thrust, exhaust velocity and gravity, no drag. The model omits gravity variation with altitude, vacuum thrust changes, staging, attitude and orbital motion. Earth/Moon changes gravity only. Parameters are educational simplifications reminiscent of Saturn V, not a reconstruction. Landing stops without rebound (estimate); impacts and damage are omitted. Samples use analytic integration on a 0.01 s grid, with exact event times.

Extra cargo choices are 0, 200000 and 500000 kg. The default is 0; Q4 compares 200000 with 500000 kg. One engine cannot lift even the dry rocket: 6900000 N is less than 9515700 N. Adding fuel cannot lower dry mass. Dashed console curves predict the current inputs; solid portions and bright cursors represent observed motion. Pre-ignition predictions are not measurements. Burnout force jumps are not joined by a vertical segment.

Assessment

  • Predict and explain a picture before manipulation.
  • Change one condition and identify fixed conditions.
  • Compare thrust, weight and support on the same cell scale.
  • Use equal-time dots and velocity tangents as evidence.
  • Distinguish immediate/delayed lift and display scale/physical quantities.

Ask: What differed between your prediction and result? Which cells or dots changed your mind? What must stay fixed to repeat the observation? What can this model not tell us?

Frequently asked questions

Does force balance mean the rocket is hovering?
On the pad, equal forces mean zero acceleration at that instant. Burning fuel immediately changes the balance. For an already moving object, zero acceleration does not mean zero velocity.
Can more fuel make one engine succeed?
No. Dry weight already exceeds its thrust.
Can students measure speed from the height pillar?
No; it is compressed. Use physical data or the linear strip.
Is the Moon easier because it has no atmosphere?
This model ignores air resistance everywhere. Only gravity changes.
Why does an idle display move?
After 90 s without input, outside busy cards and teacher mode, the kiosk cycles through three demonstrations. Touch resets to the first student quest with a hand pulse. A circular reset overlay appears after errors or five consecutive frames exceeding 1000 ms.

Three safe physical connections

  1. Pull a light block upward slowly with a spring scale and observe decreasing table support. Keep it low and away from faces. This does not reproduce fuel loss.
  2. Record a small toy cart on a low ramp at equal intervals. Add a soft stop and compare uncompressed dot spacing.
  3. Compare light and loaded carts with the same rubber-band setup over a short floor track. Wear eye protection, aim away from people, and do not claim constant rubber-band force.

Do not use fire, propellants, pressure vessels or real rocket launches.
